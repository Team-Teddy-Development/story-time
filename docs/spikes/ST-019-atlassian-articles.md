## Cross-cutting findings

### Lifecycle coverage is broader than story preparation

The hub's six-phase table includes initiation, planning, execution, review, release, and retrospective. Release includes deployment, communication, and adoption/performance monitoring; retrospective produces improvement actions. Its FAQ instead names Envision, Speculate, Explore, Adapt, and Close. These are two differently named source models, not a single authoritative six-state contract. Proposal for later skill mapping: check ownership across both delivery and learning, especially stakeholder review, release follow-up, and improvement actions. Do not infer one new skill or gate per phase. Primary source: https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management .

### Scrumban cadence and sprint commitment need an explicit local choice

The Scrumban article says teams “can't receive new tasks until the sprint ends”, yet also describes pull, continuous delivery before sprint end, and changing requirements. The methodologies article instead says to pull work as capacity allows, and sprint-cadence explicitly recognizes continuous-flow and event-based cadences. Proposal: define replenishment, stakeholder review, and retrospective cadences separately from any sprint commitment window. A sprint-free story-time design is an adaptation supported by cross-source synthesis, not a literal quotation of the Scrumban article. Primary sources: https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/scrumban , https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/project-management-methodologies , and https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/sprint-cadence .

### A simple Kanban view does not eliminate backlog or lifecycle policy

The hub says “Kanban has no backlogs (usually)” and puts waiting work in To Do. The methodologies article explicitly describes work moving from a Kanban backlog and lists a Scrumban backlog and policies. The workflow-chart article says board columns can show only part of the full workflow, while kanban-principles retains explicit policies, WIP limits, feedback, and improvement. Proposal: preserve one authoritative backlog and history while simplifying active-work views. Do not equate a three-column board with removing readiness, dependency, or acceptance rules. Primary sources: https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management , https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/project-management-methodologies , https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/workflow-chart , and https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/kanban-principles .

### Refinement, readiness, and completion have distinct purposes

Backlog grooming makes work current and actionable; refinement can be asynchronous and continuous. DoR is a team-created agreement, while DoD is a shared completion standard explicitly distinct from story-specific acceptance criteria. Proposal: revise existing backlog-groom, story-ready, and story-accept responsibilities before introducing parallel owners. A readiness score cannot substitute for understood work, and a demo or closed parent epic cannot substitute for criterion evidence. Primary sources: https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/backlog-grooming , https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/backlog-refinement-meeting , https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/definition-of-ready , https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/definition-of-done , and https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/epics .

### Estimates and velocity are forecasts, not personnel measures

Estimation concerns relative effort, risk, complexity, and uncertainty; distant work merits less precision. Fibonacci guidance supports reference stories and independent voting but warns against time conversion. Velocity uses completed work and team-local history, but does not measure quality or business value; metrics also rejects cross-team comparison. Proposal: keep optional sprint forecasting separate from WIP-limited pull, customer outcomes, and individual evaluation. Do not import points-per-person examples as quotas or rewrite historical estimates to improve charts. Primary sources: https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/estimation , https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/fibonacci-story-points , https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/velocity-scrum , and https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/metrics .

### Flow and capacity need recorded boundaries and sustainable limits

Kanban metrics distinguishes lead time from cycle time, active WIP from backlog, and throughput from point velocity. Lean pull starts with demand and available capacity; workload guidance adds skills, shared constraints, and sustainable load. Proposal: define timestamps, blocked-time treatment, and completion events before proposing reports. Use observed queues and variation for improvement, not full-utilization targets or assumptions that equal task counts mean equal load. Primary sources: https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/kanban-metrics , https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/lean-principles , https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/lean-methodology , and https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/workload-management .

### Review must change priorities, and improvement must be tested

Sprint-demo separates demonstration from broader review and requires follow-up feedback to update the backlog. Product management continues through post-launch outcomes. Continuous improvement uses measured goals and owned changes, then standardizes only after an improvement works. Proposal: check for missing ownership of stakeholder feedback, post-release learning, and retrospective experiments in the later full skill inventory. These needs do not imply three new skills if current owners can cover them. Primary sources: https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/sprint-demo , https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/product-vs-project-management , https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/continuous-improvement , and https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/lean-process-improvement .

### Planning artifacts and automation must serve actual coordination needs

Roadmaps are high-level and not necessary for every small project. Schedules and risks change with knowledge while an original baseline remains a comparison reference. Automation advice starts with mapping and removing unnecessary steps, then small pilots, observation, and controls. Vendor catalogues supply selection questions, not independent evidence that a new platform, skill, or automatic closure rule is needed. Proposal: reuse authoritative stories for status, dependencies, risks, and feedback; add a capability only after the full skill inventory establishes an uncovered outcome. Primary sources: https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/project-roadmap , https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/project-schedule , https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/project-baseline , https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/risk-analysis , https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/task-automation , https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/workflow-automation , and https://www.atlassian.com/agile/project-management/workflow-management-tools .

## Contradictions and limits for synthesis

The six-versus-five hub phase models, the Kanban backlog simplification, and the Scrumban sprint/pull tension are preserved above. Sprint-backlog and sprint-backlog-product-backlog also mix fixed sprint scope with daily updates and flexibility. Do not derive a local scope-change contract without an explicit design choice.

The source taxonomy is inconsistent. Lean-process-improvement calls Agile another Lean methodology, while lean-vs-agile calls Lean distinct from Agile. Dependency examples reverse the stated prerequisite direction in the opening landscaping example, so use explicit dependency definitions instead of copying that example. The scrum-board article labels Sprint Goal as Increment, which must not become an unchecked domain definition.

Other source-text defects include tool-catalogue count mismatches, a quarterly report described as four months, repeated metric warnings under different charts, and an Agile-value quotation rendered as “Responding to change by following a plan” in templates and waterfall-methodology. These are attributed source or extraction issues, not proposed story-time rules.

All 75 source text files were read by the assigned readers. No article access failure remains in this supplied set. Reading the extraction does not inspect images, interactive charts, video, rendering, hidden link targets, or omitted content. Incomplete source-text endings and missing workflow states remain recorded below. This contribution does not determine whether those defects originate in HTML or extraction.

Current vendor prices, product entitlements, ratings, AI claims, numerical outcomes, and security or compliance assurances were not independently validated. Supplemental Jira documentation, exhaustive installed-skill ownership, final lifecycle design, repository checks, story acceptance, and delivery remain the main agent's scope.
